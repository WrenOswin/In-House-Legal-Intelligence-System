- **A quarter to a third of all errors are boundary errors.** These predictions overlap a gold entity but do not match it exactly. Under strict matching, each one costs a false positive *and* a false negative.
- **LoRMA, the best model, makes the most errors in total.** It predicts many more entities (666 spurious) but misses far fewer (186). Its macro-recall is 0.577 against a macro-precision of 0.462; full fine-tuning is balanced (0.521 / 0.506). Macro-F1 weights rare labels (WIT, counsel, APP/RESP) as much as DATE, so the extra recall on rare labels outweighs the extra false positives on common ones.
- **Wrong-label errors are rare.** When a span's boundaries are exactly right, the label is almost always right too. Most of the loss comes from finding the exact extent, or from spurious and missed entities.

What the examples show:
- **Sub-word fragments (a major cause of "too short").** The model tags only some BPE pieces of a word: `Th` + `ukral` for "Thukral", `OF 200` for "OF 2008", `S.` for "S.B. Sinha". 97 of the best model's 2139 predictions (4.5%) start or end inside a word, against 36 of 1628 gold entities. Section 10 fixes the word-internal cases; fragments at punctuation (`S.` for "S.B. Sinha") remain.
- **Modifiers and prefixes ("too long").** `Delhi High Court` or `Division Bench of the High Court` where the gold marks only `High Court`; `O.P. No. 4612 of 1996` vs. gold `No. 4612 of 1996`.
- **Trailing punctuation.** `State Govt.` vs. gold `State Govt`; a citation followed by ` :`.
- **The annotations themselves are inconsistent** about modifiers and punctuation, which caps any strict-match score.

Three failure modes are visible:
- **Whole-document side flip.** In 1236969, 16 of the 23 respondent mentions are labelled APP: the model found the names but put them on the wrong side.
- **Mixed sides in one document.** In 154395703, the appellant's mentions are split 9 APP / 8 RESP.
- **Not found, or wrong boundary.** In 952082, 22 of 27 appellant mentions are missed or cut. Many of these are `M/s. …` company names, where the model tags fragments such as `M` (see section 7).
- **Why roles are hard:** the role is written once, in the header ("… Appellant Versus … Respondent"). Mentions thousands of tokens later look the same for both sides.
- **Tried, without improvement:** prepending the first 256 header tokens to every window (Phase 2.txt, observation 11; val 0.487–0.492 vs. 0.505–0.507 without). A document-level fix, such as propagating the role decided in the header to later mentions of the same name, is the natural next step.

- **AUTH is the biggest source of false positives (333 of 666).** Generic institutional words (`Government`, `State`, `Board`, `University`) and organisation acronyms (`AICTE`, `VESCO`) are tagged in documents where the gold does not mark them at all. Within a document the gold is consistent: every standalone repeat of an annotated string is annotated (Phase 2.txt, observation 4), so these are documents where the annotators did not treat the word as an entity.
- **Fragments add to it:** `M` from "M/s." is predicted as AUTH or RESP repeatedly.
- **Some "spurious" predictions look like plausible entities** (`Constitution`, `Trade Marks Act, 1999`) but are not marked in that document. Strict scoring counts them as errors.

- **No clear position effect.** Recall stays between 0.59 and 0.69 across the document. The first 20% is not better even though it contains the header; it holds most of the hard party and counsel labels.

- **Sliding windows cover every token.** Windows are 1,024 tokens for A/C, 768 for B and 2,048 for D, with 50% overlap. Each token keeps the prediction from the window where it is most central, so no entity is cut by a window edge.

## 9. Ceiling of the token pipeline

Converting gold spans to BIO tags and back gives macro-F1 **0.9944** (0.9875 with the word snapping from section 10, which wrongly extends the few gold entities that end inside a word, e.g. "Railway Board" in "Railway Boards"). The tokenization is not what limits the scores above.

## 10. Fix found by this analysis: snap predictions to word boundaries

Gold entities almost never start or end inside a word, but the models' predictions sometimes do (section 4). Extending every predicted span to whole words, then merging spans that now overlap, improves **every** model without retraining (validation, same predictions as above):

| Experiment | Val macro-F1 | + word snapping | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| E2 LoRMA (A 140M) | 0.5074 | 0.5185 | +0.0111 |
| E1 full FT, bidir (A 140M) | 0.4728 | 0.4883 | +0.0155 |
| E1 full FT, causal (A 140M) | 0.5048 | 0.5112 | +0.0064 |
| E6 KD student (A 58M) | 0.4771 | 0.4846 | +0.0075 |
| E6 student alone (A 58M) | 0.4510 | 0.4645 | +0.0135 |
| E5 partial FT (D 54M) | 0.4614 | 0.4764 | +0.0150 |
| E4 adapters (C 58M) | 0.4030 | 0.4076 | +0.0047 |
| E3 LoRA (B 42M) | 0.3198 | 0.3369 | +0.0171 |

- **Confirmed on the test set:** the same LoRMA model scores **0.5174** on Codabench with snapping, against 0.5090 without (+0.008). This is the best Phase 2 result.
- **Snapping is now part of decoding** (`ner_data.snap_to_words`).
