## 5. Findings

1. **The models fall back on the majority role.** RatioOfTheDecision is predicted 710 times for 403 gold sentences; Fact, precedents, arguments and lower-court rulings are all pulled into it. The rarer roles are under-predicted: RulingByLowerCourt 26/102, None 13/33, Issue 6/15. Macro-F1 weights these rare roles as much as Ratio, so this is where the score is lost.
2. **Closely related roles are the hardest pairs:** ArgumentPetitioner vs ArgumentRespondent (the wording of the two sides' arguments is the same; only who is arguing differs), PrecedentReliedUpon vs Fact/Ratio (a cited case is reasoning or background depending on how the court uses it), and RulingByLowerCourt vs Ratio/Fact (a lower court's decision is described in the same language as the present court's).
3. **Whole segments get the wrong role.** The error rate is higher next to a gold role change (0.48) than inside a run (0.35), but most wrong sentences (607 of 800) sit inside runs, and the predicted sequences change role about as often as the gold ones (253 vs 249): the BiGRU/CRF finds the segments but labels whole segments with the wrong role. The 'previous gold role' oracle reaches 0.638 all-13 macro-F1 on dev, more than any model (best single run 0.479).
4. **Domain:** IT scores higher than Corporate Law for 4 of the 6 main models on dev (R2 is better on CL, R3 ties; fig 4), and for the ensemble (0.475 vs 0.399). Part of the gap is arithmetic: the CL dev documents contain only 10 of the 13 roles (no PrecedentNotReliedUpon), so their all-13 average is capped lower, and CL has only 3 Issue sentences (F1 0). CL documents are also longer (dev median 184 vs 120 sentences) and dominated by long argument sections where the two sides alternate (ArgPet/ArgResp).
5. **Noise:** 10 dev and 10 test documents. Codabench − dev = +0.009 ± 0.037 over the 15 submissions with a dev score (Pearson r 0.37), submissions, and the same configuration trained on 10 more documents moved by −0.10 to +0.02. Differences below ~0.05 between systems are not resolvable; the robust findings are the architecture gap (A/D/C vs B) and that parameter-efficient methods match full fine-tuning (LoRMA 0.450–0.476 vs full FT 0.415 on Codabench), both also seen in Phase 2.
6. **Logit adjustment** (subtracting 0.5·log prior to favour rare roles) gave +0.01 on dev but −0.009 on average on Codabench (4 submissions): it does not reliably fix finding 1.
